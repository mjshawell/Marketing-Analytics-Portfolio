Employee performance by attribute

  • Employee performance averages 4.21/5, with good internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.866).

  • Courtesy and helpfulness are the strongest employee attributes, receiving average ratings of 4.35 and 4.31, respectively.

  • Professionalism and availability both average 4.08/5, representing relative opportunities for improvement.

  • Savings rate perceptions average 3.07/5 after reverse coding and excluding no-opinion responses.

  • 59.4% agree or strongly agree that the credit union meets member needs.

  • Only 29.7% agree or strongly agree that the credit union offers lower rates.

Strengthen competitive positioning

Investigate how members perceive the credit union’s interest rates relative to competing financial institutions. Communicate verified financial benefits more clearly.

Maintain employee service quality

Preserve the credit union’s strengths in employee courtesy and helpfulness while identifying opportunities to improve professionalism and availability.

Improve member satisfaction

Conduct additional research to identify unmet financial needs and develop targeted service improvements.

Members in the headquarters area report slightly higher employee performance ratings (4.28) compared with members elsewhere (4.14).

Member needs satisfaction is nearly identical between the two geographic groups, with ratings of 3.60 and 3.59, respectively.

Savings rate perceptions are also similar, averaging 3.04 in the headquarters area and 3.09 elsewhere.

These findings suggest relatively consistent perceptions across the two geographic segments. However, the differences are descriptive and have not been evaluated through statistical significance testing.

Q38 is labeled “headquarter” in the dataset. The geographic interpretation follows the working analysis plan and should be verified against the original questionnaire.

The purpose of this dashboard is to demonstrate the application of marketing analytics and customer insights research to credit union member satisfaction, financial product awareness, employee performance, and geographic segmentation.

This academic project uses Case 31 as an alternative to confidential MSDM Culminating Experience Project data.

The dashboard translates statistical results into accessible and interactive visualizations that support managerial decision-making.

The original dataset was imported using the haven package. Data preparation and aggregation were performed using dplyr and tidyr.

The dashboard contains aggregate analytical results rather than individual survey records.

Employee performance

Employee performance was calculated as the mean of Q3–Q6, representing courtesy, helpfulness, professionalism, and availability.

The calculated composite matched the existing employee performance variable exactly.

Savings rate perceptions

Q7 was reverse coded using the following transformation:

\[\text{Savings Rate Score} = 6 - Q7\]

Missing responses and no-opinion responses were excluded.

  • Q28 and Q29 are absent from the supplied dataset.

  • Response code 3 for Q13–Q26 is not labeled in the SPSS metadata.

  • Missing and non-substantive responses were excluded from relevant rating calculations.

  • Favorable ratings for Q30 and Q33 represent responses of 4 or 5 among valid responses.

  • Geographic comparisons are descriptive rather than inferential.

  • No causal relationships or statistical significance claims are presented.

  • Permission to publish the aggregate case results should be confirmed before public publication.
